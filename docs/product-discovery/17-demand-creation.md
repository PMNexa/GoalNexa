# 17 — Demand Creation: Mapping Concepts to Noncustomers

For each of the 20 concepts in [14 — Blue Ocean Concepts](14-blue-ocean-concepts.md), which noncustomer tier from [10 — Noncustomers](10-noncustomers.md) it could convert, and what new demand — demand that doesn't exist for any current competitor in [05 — Competitor Map](05-competitor-map.md) — it creates. All entries are HYPOTHESES; none of this is validated demand, only a reasoned mapping from evidence already gathered.

| # | Concept | Tier converted | New demand created |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Homelab Widget | Tier 3 — self-hosting hobbyists | People who'd never install a dedicated "tracker app" but would flip on a widget already inside a dashboard routine they run daily — demand for *presence*, not a new destination |
| 2 | Stake & Self-Host | Tier 2 — gamification-averse who know cosmetic mechanics don't work for them | People who've specifically rejected points/streaks but never found a self-hosted version of real financial stakes |
| 3 | One Instance, Two Layers | Tier 3 — solopreneurs/tiny teams (the sub-5-person gap, [12](12-strategic-groups.md)) | Founders who don't bother with any goal software today because running two separate tools for "my habits" and "our OKRs" isn't worth it at their scale |
| 4 | Auto-Habit passive capture | Tier 2 — people who abandon trackers specifically due to manual-logging friction | People who already generate the relevant data elsewhere (Git commits, Home Assistant, fitness trackers) but never manually re-enter it into a tracker |
| 5 | Bus-Factor Dashboard | Tier 1 (SaaS refugees fearing vendor risk) + Tier 3 (self-hosters wary of small OSS projects specifically) | Risk-averse adopters blocked not by self-hosting itself but by fear the *specific project* will be abandoned — a distinct objection from general self-hosting hesitancy |
| 6 | Migrate-from-Notion Wizard | Tier 3 — Notion/Sheets/Obsidian template users ([04](04-current-solutions.md)) | People satisfied enough with their spreadsheet to never search for "goal tracker" at all, who'd switch only if the migration cost were near-zero |
| 7 | Federated Accountability Pods | Tier 3 — self-hosting friend groups | Groups where everyone already self-hosts independently and want shared challenges without any one person's instance (or a company) becoming the de facto center |
| 8 | SSO-Native by Default | Tier 3 — hobbyists with an existing Authelia/Authentik stack | Hobbyists who treat "doesn't integrate with my identity provider" as a hard rejection filter, not a nice-to-have |
| 9 | Notify, Don't Nag (Apprise) | Tier 3 — same audience as #8 | Same population, converted by removing yet another proprietary mobile-app notification permission from their life |
| 10 | Pay What You Fail (charity) | Tier 2 — ethically motivated rejecters of Beeminder's model | People who like the stakes mechanism in principle but are specifically uncomfortable with a company profiting from their personal failure |
| 11 | Git-Native Goal Templates | Tier 3 — participants in the existing Notion-template economy ([04](04-current-solutions.md)) | Template creators/consumers already active in that market who'd prefer a portable, non-proprietary format over Gumroad-locked Notion templates |
| 12 | Compliance Mode | Tier 1 — the untested regulated-industry sub-segment flagged in [06](06-pressure-test.md) | Teams with a *hard* data-residency requirement (not just a preference) who were never reachable by general-purpose self-hosted OKR tools lacking audit/compliance documentation |
| 13 | Managed-Install Consultancy Kit | Tier 3 — non-technical small businesses ([06](06-pressure-test.md) HN thread) | An entire buyer type — the business itself, mediated through a partner — who would never touch Docker directly but will pay someone else to |
| 14 | Data Ownership Passport | Tier 1 — SaaS refugees already burned by lock-in once | People willing to adopt self-hosted software generally but specifically wary of *repeating* the lock-in experience with a self-hosted maintainer's proprietary format |
| 15 | Quantified Team (zero-input OKRs) | Tier 1 (churned OKR-tool users) + Tier 3 (teams with no formal tool) | Teams who tried OKR software before and dropped it specifically because manual updates felt like overhead nobody kept up with |
| 16 | Streakless Mode | Tier 2 — gamification-averse, non-financial-stakes-averse | People who reject both cosmetic gamification *and* financial stakes but still want visibility into their own trend — an underserved middle this research found no competitor occupying |
| 17 | Sponsor-a-Feature Roadmap | Tier 1 — existing OSS-tracker users used to being ignored by a volunteer maintainer's roadmap | Not a noncustomer conversion so much as a loyalty/monetization play among people already self-hosting a comparable tool who want a say in its direction |
| 18 | Solopreneur Tier | Tier 3 — the 1-4 person gap identified in [12 — Strategic Groups](12-strategic-groups.md) | The specific segment strategic-group analysis found underserved by every existing competitor, including Operately |
| 19 | Ambient Physical Display | Tier 2 (screen-fatigue rejecters) + Tier 3 (hardware tinkerers who already own e-ink/Pi gear) | People who've rejected app-based tracking specifically because it's one more screen to check, converted by removing the screen-checking step entirely |
| 20 | Verified Referee Network | Tier 2 (distrust a central company as referee) + Tier 3 (federated self-hosters) | People who like the StickK/Beeminder referee mechanic but reject a company holding that role — demand for the mechanism without the intermediary |

## Synthesis

INSIGHT — Tier 3 (self-hosting hobbyists who've never extended their practice to goal tracking, and Notion/Sheets/Obsidian users who've never looked for dedicated software) is targeted by the largest number of concepts (11 of 20) and is the tier [10 — Noncustomers](10-noncustomers.md) already flagged as the most reachable and least contested. Tier 2's gamification-averse and screen-fatigue sub-segments are the next-most-addressable and are notably **not served by any competitor identified in [05 — Competitor Map](05-competitor-map.md)** — every existing product assumes some form of active engagement with a screen-based app, which concepts #16, #19, and #4 all directly break from. Tier 2's largest sub-segment — the "do nothing" / rapid-abandonment majority ([04](04-current-solutions.md), ~80% resolution-failure rate) — remains, consistent with [10 — Noncustomers](10-noncustomers.md)'s original read, out of reach for any tracking product: none of these 20 concepts claim to solve sustained motivation itself, only to remove friction or change the mechanism for people who already have some intent to track.

**RECOMMENDATION:** if forced to prioritize, concepts converting Tier 3 (#1, #6, #7, #8, #9, #11, #18) carry the least validation risk — they extend a behavior (self-hosting, template-building) this exact audience is already proven to practice, rather than asking anyone to adopt a wholly new behavior (financial stakes, ambient hardware) with no evidence of appetite yet gathered in this research.
